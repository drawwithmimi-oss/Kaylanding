Why This Workshop

Sometimes the strongest teams need the simplest tools

The challenge you know too well

Another team off-site. Another round of icebreakers. Another presentation about psychological safety. People nod politely, check their phones, and leave unchanged.

What actually works

A box of crayons, the right prompts, and someone who knows how to hold the room.

Kay has been using crayons with teams since she was handed a box on a psych unit at 18. The pattern is consistent: when people draw instead of talk first, something different happens. Defenses drop. Real conversations start. The takeaway sticks.

This workshop is designed to feel light and land deep.

The Workshop

Play as a serious tool

What it’s about

Crayons bypass the analytical brain. They invite play. They give people permission to express what words can’t. Kay learned this on a psych unit at 18, and has been using crayons with teams ever since.

This is a facilitated creative session with no art skills required. Participants reflect, create, and share. The result: stronger connection, unexpected insights, and a team that remembers this session long after it ends.

“The crayon box method is simple on purpose. That’s why it works.”

Great for off-sites, retreats, ERG events, team kickoffs, and wellness days.

The Experience

How the session flows

A tight, intentional arc. Kay adapts the timing and prompts to your group.

Step 01

Ground

A short story and grounding exercise. Permission to slow down.

Step 02

Create

Guided prompts. Crayons. Everyone draws. No talent required.

Step 03

Reflect

Small-group or paired conversations about what surfaced.

Step 04

Apply

Close with a concrete takeaway for the team or individual.

What teams walk away with

Deeper trust

The shared vulnerability of a simple creative act surfaces connection fast.

Fresh perspective

People see their own work and relationships differently after drawing them.

Shared language

The “crayon box” becomes a team reference point long after the workshop.

Energy reset

A genuine break from screens and slides that teams actually look forward to.
